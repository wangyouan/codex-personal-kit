# English Finance module

Venues include *Journal of Finance, Journal of Financial Economics, Review of Financial Studies, JFQA, Review of Finance,* and *Journal of Corporate Finance*. Per the source author's preference, also use this module for *Management Science, Strategic Management Journal,* and *Academy of Management Journal*. The patterns are distilled from roughly 1,000 finance articles in the source library.

Reproduce the logic and register, never a corpus sentence.

## Register

Finance prose is confident, empirically anchored, and economically motivated. Sentences should connect estimates to a mechanism rather than report correlations in isolation. First-person plural is common. Use present tense for findings and past tense for completed procedures. Calibrate hedging to identification strength.

## Abstract

A common empirical abstract contains six to ten sentences:

1. Give a one-line motivation, tension, or puzzle.
2. State the question, setting, and identification strategy.
3. Report the headline result with sign and economic magnitude.
4. Identify important heterogeneity or who is affected.
5. Present evidence on the mechanism.
6. Close with the economic takeaway.

For a theory or analytical paper, move from model and decision problem to the central result, comparative statics, and an empirical implication.

Do not add citations to an abstract unless the target journal explicitly permits them.

## Introduction: the finance funnel

A common introduction uses these moves, with flexible length:

1. **Hook:** stakes, puzzle, concrete fact, or short real-world setting.
2. **Question:** state early what the paper studies.
3. **Why the answer is open:** explain what existing evidence cannot resolve.
4. **Approach:** introduce data, setting, and identification design.
5. **Identifying assumption:** state it openly and summarize evidence on pre-trends, placebos, or alternative explanations.
6. **Findings:** main effect, magnitude, heterogeneity, mechanism, and robustness in logical order.
7. **Contribution:** position against the closest two or three literatures and delimit external validity.

Competing predictions are often useful: explain why the sign is ambiguous and what evidence can distinguish the channels. Do not manufacture a two-sided tension when theory is not genuinely ambiguous.

## Section architecture

Typical empirical structure:

1. Introduction
2. Institutional background or setting
3. Data, sample, and measurement
4. Empirical strategy and identifying assumption
5. Main results
6. Mechanisms or cross-sectional tests
7. Robustness, often with details in an appendix
8. Conclusion

Hypotheses are more common in management and some corporate-finance outlets than in JF/JFE-style papers.

## Results register

- Lead with the estimate and explain its economic magnitude.
- Use “consistent with” for mechanism evidence that does not uniquely identify the channel.
- State nulls cleanly.
- Describe tables selectively; do not narrate every cell.
- Use active voice for robustness and identification checks.
- Separate statistical significance from economic importance.
- Do not call a policy or shock “exogenous” without defending that claim.

## Contribution and positioning

Useful rhetorical functions include:

- identify what the design reveals that earlier data could not;
- distinguish the paper from the closest studies rather than list broad literatures;
- explain how the mechanism changes interpretation of the main effect;
- connect the findings to policy or practice only after establishing the evidence.

Use “first” claims sparingly and only after verification.

## Referee lens

Assess:

1. **Motivation:** is there an economic question or only a new correlation?
2. **Identification:** is the shock plausibly exogenous; is the identifying assumption explicit; are pre-trends, placebos, and alternative channels addressed?
3. **Magnitude:** are the effects economically meaningful?
4. **Mechanism:** is the proposed channel tested rather than asserted?
5. **Contribution:** is the paper positioned against the closest work without overselling?
6. **Exposition:** does the introduction deliver the question, design, and results early enough?

Deliver a short summary, major comments, minor comments, and the changes most likely to move the paper up a tier.

## Register cues

Common, when accurate: “consistent with,” “plausibly exogenous,” “economically meaningful,” “we rule out,” “speaks to,” “staggered adoption,” and “identifying assumption.”

Avoid empty connector chains, “plays a crucial role,” “delve into,” “in today's world,” repeated “shed light on,” and stacked hedges.
