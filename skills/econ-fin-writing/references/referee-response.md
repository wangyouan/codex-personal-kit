# Referee response

Use for point-by-point responses to editors and referees in English or Chinese. A strong response resolves the concern rather than winning an argument: acknowledge, act or clarify, and identify the revision.

## Full response structure

1. **Editor cover note:** thank the editor and referees; summarize two to four material revisions; state that the detailed response follows.
2. **Per-referee opening:** briefly acknowledge how the report improved the paper.
3. **Numbered comments:** label comments consistently, such as R1.1, R1.2, and R2.1.
4. **Response blocks:** visually separate the referee's text from the authors' response.
5. **Verification pointers:** cite confirmed sections, tables, figures, pages, paragraphs, or quoted new text.
6. **Closing:** end with a brief thanks.

## Moves for a single comment

- **Agree and act:** “We agree. We now [action]. See [verified location].”
- **Agree in part:** acknowledge the underlying concern, explain the constraint, and describe the feasible change that addresses it.
- **Clarify a misunderstanding:** attribute the confusion to the manuscript's earlier exposition and revise the paper to prevent it.
- **Push back carefully:** explain the methodological or conceptual reason, add a feasible alternative analysis or clarification, and let readers assess the evidence.
- **Report additional analysis:** state the specification, result, and location accurately, including a null when that is what the analysis finds.
- **Acknowledge an unfixable limitation:** explain the boundary and add a candid limitation to the paper.

Do not repeat the same thank-you sentence for every comment. Keep the tone warm, concise, and professional.

## Completed versus proposed work

Use completed language only after checking the revised manuscript or analysis:

- Completed: “We added Table A7 and discuss it in Section 5.2.”
- Proposed: “We propose to add the analysis to the appendix.”
- Unknown location: “See [location to confirm].”
- Unknown result: “This analysis would test whether [threat]; its result must be inserted after estimation.”

Never infer that a regression, robustness test, or rewrite has been completed from the referee response alone.

## Make the response easy to verify

For every material comment, answer:

- What concern did the referee raise?
- What changed?
- Why does the change address the concern?
- Where can the change be found?
- Does any limitation remain?

Quote new manuscript text when it is short. For a long change, summarize it and point precisely to the revision.

## Register

- Use first-person plural.
- Avoid defensiveness and inflated flattery.
- Give a substantive concession before an explanation when the concern is valid.
- Tie each action to the referee's stated threat.
- Match field conventions: identification threats in economics and finance, construct and measurement validity in accounting, and parallel trends, concurrent policies, or measure validation in Chinese empirical work.

## 中文回复

- 使用礼貌、简洁的学术语体：“感谢审稿专家指出这一重要问题”“前稿对此说明不够清晰，我们已作如下修改。”
- 逐条编号，先复述意见，再给出答复与修改位置。
- 需要澄清时，把误解归因于前稿表述，而不是审稿人。
- 区分“已经修改”和“拟作修改”，未知位置用 `[待核对页码/章节]`。
- 结尾简短致谢，避免反复使用同一套客气话。

## Common failures

- “We have revised accordingly” without saying how or where.
- Long arguments that never change the paper.
- Claiming an analysis is complete without checking it.
- Hiding a null or limitation.
- Quoting a location that does not exist in the current draft.
- Responding to a different, easier concern than the one raised.
